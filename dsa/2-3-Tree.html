2-3 Tree

A 2-3 Tree is a special type of B-tree (specifically of minimum degree/order 3) in which every internal node is either a **2-node** (holding 1 key and 2 children) or a **3-node** (holding 2 keys and 3 children), and every leaf node sits at the exact same depth (`perfectly_balanced`).

Imagine a road signpost that can either hold one town name splitting traffic Left and Right (smaller or larger), or two town names splitting traffic Left, Middle, and Right (smaller than both, in between the two, or larger than both). Whenever a signpost gets a third town name crammed onto it, it splits cleanly in two and sends its middle name up to the signpost above it.


Understanding the Basic Terminology

2-Node: A standard binary-style node containing 1 key ($K_1$) and 2 child branches (values $< K_1$, and values $> K_1$).

3-Node (`max_degree_3` / `multi_way_branching`): A wider node containing 2 sorted keys ($K_1 < K_2$) and 3 child branches (values $< K_1$, values between $K_1$ and $K_2$, and values $> K_2$).

Temporary 4-Node Split: When inserting into a 3-node briefly creates 3 keys, the node splits into two 2-nodes and pushes the middle key up to its parent.

Perfect Balance (`perfectly_balanced`): Because splits only ever create a new root at the top when the whole tree fills up, all root-to-leaf paths have the exact same length.


How Does a 2-3 Tree Work Conceptually?

Unlike a standard binary search tree that grows downward by attaching new leaves at the bottom (which risks making one branch too long), a 2-3 tree absorbs new insertions into existing bottom nodes first. Turning a 2-node into a 3-node doesn't change the tree's height at all.

When a 3-node overflows, its middle key bubbles upward. Even if this chain reaction reaches the root and splits the root itself, the entire tree grows taller by one level uniformly across every single branch at once.


Where Are 2-3 Trees Used in the Real World?

Teaching Self-Balancing Multi-Way Trees: Provide the cleanest, most intuitive model for understanding how B-trees split and merge without drowning in hundreds of keys.

Conceptual Blueprint for Left-Leaning Red-Black Trees: Directly inspire simplified Red-Black tree implementations where red links simulate 3-nodes.

In-Memory Ordered Maps and Finger Trees: Power functional data structures in languages like Haskell and Scala.


Summary

A 2-3 tree is the compact, perfectly balanced ancestor of the B-tree family, using flexible 2-way and 3-way nodes to grow upward from the root without ever losing its balance.
