Deque

A Deque (pronounced "deck", short for **Double-Ended Queue**) is a special type of queue that removes the one-way restriction, allowing elements to be added and removed from *both* the front and the back ends. It acts as a hybrid combining the powers of a stack and a queue in a single structure.

Imagine a narrow ferry boat with loading ramps at both the bow (front) and the stern (back). Cars can drive onto the ferry from either ramp, and they can drive off from either ramp as needed. A deque provides this exact two-ended freedom for data.


Understanding the Basic Terminology

Push Front / Push Back: Inserting a new item at the very beginning or the very end of the deque.

Pop Front / Pop Back: Removing an existing item from either the front or the back end.

Double-Ended Access: The ability to treat the structure as a FIFO queue (push back, pop front), a LIFO stack (push back, pop back), or both simultaneously.


How Does a Deque Work Conceptually?

Usually built using either a circular dynamic array or a doubly linked list, a deque keeps active pointers to both ends of the collection. Every addition or removal at either extremity takes constant time. You can push urgent items straight to the front of the line while regular items join at the back, or drop expired items off the back when the structure gets too long.


Where Are Deques Used in the Real World?

Work-Stealing Task Schedulers: Let a processor core grab tasks from the front of its own deque, while idle cores "steal" waiting tasks from the back of busy cores' deques.

Sliding Window Algorithms: Track the maximum or minimum stock price over a moving time window by adding new prices at one end and evicting old ones from the other.

Capped Undo History: Push new user actions onto the front while automatically dropping the oldest actions off the back once history exceeds 100 steps.


Summary

A deque is a versatile, two-door waiting line. By opening both the front and the back for instant additions and removals, it handles stacks, queues, and sliding windows in one unified structure.
