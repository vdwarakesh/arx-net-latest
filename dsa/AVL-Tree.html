AVL Tree

An AVL Tree is a type of Binary Search Tree (BST) that keeps itself balanced as values are inserted or removed. It prevents the tree from becoming excessively tilted to one side, helping maintain efficient data access.

Its defining feature is the balance factor, which measures the difference between the heights of a node’s left and right subtrees. For every node, this difference must be -1, 0, or +1. When an update disrupts this balance, the tree rearranges some of its connections through a process called rotation.

Imagine a bookshelf where books are organized by number. If too many books end up arranged along one side, the shelves are reorganized to keep the overall arrangement balanced and accessible.


Where Is It Useful?

AVL trees are useful in applications that require frequent lookups and consistently balanced data, such as in-memory indexes, dictionaries, and ordered collections.


Summary

An AVL Tree is a self-balancing BST that prioritizes strict balance to keep its structure from becoming lopsided.
