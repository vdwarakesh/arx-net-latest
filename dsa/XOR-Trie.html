XOR Trie

An XOR Trie is a type of Trie that organizes the binary representations of numbers, usually one bit at a time, rather than organizing words character by character.

Each level represents a bit position, and each node branches according to whether that bit is 0 or 1. This arrangement is particularly useful for problems involving the bitwise XOR operation, which compares corresponding bits and produces 1 when they differ and 0 when they match.

For example, XOR-based problems often ask which number in a collection produces the largest XOR value when combined with a given number. An XOR Trie can use the binary structure to guide the search toward a promising candidate bit by bit.

Imagine choosing a route through a series of doors labeled 0 and 1. At each step, you choose the door that best satisfies the goal of maximizing the difference between your binary number and a stored number, according to XOR rules.


Where Is It Useful?

XOR Tries are useful in competitive programming, maximum-XOR queries, finding pairs of numbers with large XOR values, and specialized bitwise data-processing tasks.


Summary

An XOR Trie stores numbers by their bits, creating a structure tailored to answering questions about XOR relationships rather than ordinary alphabetical or numerical ordering.
