Binary Heap

A Binary Heap is a type of Heap that uses a complete binary tree structure to organize its elements according to a heap-ordering rule.

Its shape is carefully maintained: every level is filled before the next level begins, except that the final level may be partially filled from left to right. The ordering rule ensures that each parent has the appropriate priority relationship with its children.

A binary heap is commonly stored in an array rather than using separate node objects and links. This makes its representation compact and practical for computer programs.

Imagine a tournament where players are arranged in successive rounds, with each position connected to the positions directly below it. The arrangement follows a consistent structure, while the priority rule determines how competitors are positioned relative to one another.


Where Is It Useful?

Binary heaps are commonly used to build priority queues, schedule tasks, and manage collections where the highest- or lowest-priority element must be accessed repeatedly.


Summary

A Binary Heap combines a predictable, compact structure with a priority-ordering rule, making it a practical way to manage prioritized data.
