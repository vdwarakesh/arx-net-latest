Treap

A Treap is a type of Binary Search Tree (BST) that combines two ordering rules: one based on the stored values and another based on assigned priorities.

Each node contains a value and a priority. The values follow the BST ordering rule, while the priorities follow a heap-ordering rule. Together, these rules determine the tree's arrangement.

Priorities are often assigned randomly, which helps the tree maintain a reasonably balanced shape in typical situations without relying on a strict height-balancing condition.

Imagine organizing a competition where every participant has a unique name and a randomly assigned ranking number. Names determine where participants belong alphabetically, while their ranking numbers influence their positions in the overall hierarchy. The two rules work together to shape the arrangement.


Where Is It Useful?

Treaps are useful in ordered collections, randomized data structures, and applications that need a relatively simple way to maintain sorted values with flexible structural changes.


Summary

A Treap blends BST ordering with priority-based ordering, using two complementary rules to organize its nodes.
