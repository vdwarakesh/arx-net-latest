Trie

A Trie is a tree-based data structure designed to store and retrieve strings by organizing them around their shared prefixes. Instead of treating each word as one indivisible value, it represents words through paths formed by their characters.

For example, the words “cat,” “car,” and “care” share the prefix “ca.” A Trie can share the path for those common characters and branch when the words begin to differ. A marker indicates where a complete word ends, distinguishing a stored word from a prefix that continues into other words.

Imagine a dictionary where words are organized letter by letter. When you type “ca” into a search bar, the system can use that shared beginning to narrow down possible suggestions.


Where Is It Useful?

Tries are used in autocomplete systems, search suggestions, spell-checking tools, dictionaries, and applications that need to locate strings by their prefixes.


Summary

A Trie organizes strings around shared prefixes, making it especially useful when matching the beginnings of words matters more than treating each word as an unrelated item.
