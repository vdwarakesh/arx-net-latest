Li Chao Tree

A Li Chao Tree is a specialized tree-based data structure designed to maintain a collection of linear functions and efficiently determine which function produces the minimum or maximum value at a given point.

A linear function describes a straight line, commonly written as y = mx + b, where m is the slope and b is the intercept. Different lines may produce the best result at different positions. A Li Chao Tree organizes these lines so that queries can identify the best available value at a particular coordinate.

Imagine a delivery company comparing several pricing plans. Each plan has a fixed starting cost and a different cost per kilometer. The cheapest plan may depend on the delivery distance. Rather than comparing every plan from scratch for every distance, the company needs a structure that helps identify the cheapest option at the requested point.

That is the kind of problem a Li Chao Tree is designed to handle. It is particularly useful when lines are added over time and queries ask for the minimum or maximum value at a particular coordinate.


Where Is It Useful?

Li Chao Trees are used in competitive programming, dynamic optimization problems, cost-minimization models, and certain scheduling and planning problems involving linear functions.


Summary

A Li Chao Tree organizes linear functions to help identify the best-performing line at a given point, making it useful for problems where the optimal choice changes with the input value.
