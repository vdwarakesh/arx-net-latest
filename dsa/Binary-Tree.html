Binary Tree

A Binary Tree is a type of tree in which each node can have at most two children. These are commonly referred to as the left child and the right child. A node may have no children, only one child, or both children.

Think of a series of yes-or-no questions. At each step, you choose between two possible directions: yes or no. Each answer leads you to another question or eventually to a final decision. A binary tree organizes these choices into a branching structure where each decision point has no more than two possible branches.

One important detail: “At most two children” does not mean every node must have exactly two children. A node with just one child, or even no children, is perfectly valid in a binary tree.


Common Forms of Binary Trees

Full Binary Tree: A tree in which every node has either exactly two children or no children.

Complete Binary Tree: A tree in which every level is filled except possibly the last, and the last level is filled from left to right.

Perfect Binary Tree: A tree in which every internal node has exactly two children, and all leaf nodes are at the same depth.

These terms describe different structural arrangements of binary trees.


Where Are Binary Trees Useful?

Expression Representation: Mathematical expressions such as (A + B) × C can be represented as a tree, showing how the individual operations relate to one another.

Decision Systems: A simple troubleshooting tool can ask a sequence of two-option questions to narrow down a problem.

Data Organization: Binary trees provide a foundation for specialized structures used to organize, search, and manage information.

Game Logic: In games with two possible choices at each stage, a binary tree can represent possible decisions and their resulting outcomes.


Summary

A binary tree is a branching structure where every node has a maximum of two children. Think of it as a series of two-way doors: at each point, you can move left, move right, or reach the end of the path.
