Euler Tour Tree

An Euler Tour Tree (ETT) is a special type of dynamic tree data structure that represents an entire tree by flattening its **Euler tour**—the cyclic walk that traverses every edge once downward and once upward—into a balanced binary search tree (such as a Treap or Splay Tree), enabling fast **subtree aggregations** along with dynamic link and cut operations.

Imagine walking around the outside perimeter of a fenced garden shaped like a branching tree, keeping your right hand touching the fence the entire time until you return to the front gate. Notice that whenever you step into a branch, you trace that entire sub-branch completely before stepping back out. Because of that, every subtree in the garden corresponds to one contiguous, unbroken stretch of your perimeter walk!


Understanding the Basic Terminology

Euler Tour Sequence: The sequence of vertices (or directed edges) visited during a depth-first walk around the tree, recording each node both when entering its subtree and when returning from each child.

Subtree Contiguity: Every subtree rooted at $v$ appears as one contiguous interval between the *first* appearance of $v$ and the *last* appearance of $v$ in the flattened tour sequence.

Cyclic Shift (Re-Rooting): Changing the root of the tree simply by cutting the Euler tour sequence before the new root's first occurrence and pasting the front slice onto the back.


How Does an Euler Tour Tree Work Conceptually?

While a Link-Cut Tree excels at *path* queries (from a node to the root), it struggles with *subtree* queries when a node has thousands of children. An Euler Tour Tree solves this by storing the flattened Euler tour inside a balanced sequence BST (like an Implicit Treap or Splay Tree).

Cutting an edge between $u$ and $v$ simply means slicing the contiguous segment of $v$'s subtree out of $u$'s tour and stitching the two outer ends of $u$'s tour back together (`Split` and `Merge`). Linking two trees is just splicing one tour into the middle of another—all in $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ time.


Where Are Euler Tour Trees Used in the Real World?

Fully Dynamic Graph Connectivity (Holm-de Lichtenberg-Thorup Algorithm): Maintain a spanning forest of a graph where edges are both inserted and deleted, checking component sizes in $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ time.

Dynamic Subtree Aggregations: Compute the sum, minimum, or node count of any whole subtree while the tree's root and edges are dynamically rewired.


Summary

An Euler Tour Tree flattens a tree's perimeter walk into a balanced sequence tree, turning dynamic tree links, cuts, re-rootings, and whole-subtree queries into simple array-slice splits and merges.
